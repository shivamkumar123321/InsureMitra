# D04 — Coverage guidance and validation

## Status

**Accepted with owner-selected fallback, 2026-10-26.** Source-backed coverage options and safe input-validation boundaries are documented. The user selected ₹20 lakh as the default starting suggestion when budget or a matching quote is missing, because of concern about hospital costs and inflation. This is an owner-selected product default, not an official universal adequacy standard. Do not call it sufficient for every person/family or claim that a ₹20 lakh bill will be fully paid. Exact plan eligibility, restrictions and the available base-SI choice still control.

## Evidence-based policy for supported choices

- Build coverage options from the exact product, variant, policy structure, and covered-member configuration that has passed the catalogue's source/eligibility checks. Never combine a per-person SI with a per-policy floater SI or combine different product structures into a common option.
- Show 2–3 amounts that the applicable official policy source actually offers, naming the policy and structure. If fewer than two supported choices exist, show the available choices without padding. If the selected product offers a named “Unlimited Sum Insured” option, display it as that distinct option; do not turn it into a numeric amount or compare it as a finite SI.
- For one selected policy option, distinguish base Sum Insured from bonuses, restoration, multipliers, and other benefits. Do not present a multiplier/restoration headline as the selected base SI.
- Family composition and health needs constrain which documented policy structures/options can be offered. Keep unknown eligibility/terms explicit and do not suggest an option as suitable until relevant documented rules are checked.
- Do not label any amount “adequate”, “enough”, or a payout guarantee. No general amount-per-person, income multiple, medical-cost multiplier, city-tier formula, or family-size formula is supported by this evidence.

## Premium and affordability behavior

- A quoted premium is comparable to the user's budget only when the quote matches the relevant plan/variant, covered members and ages, location/zone when applicable, policy structure, base SI, tenure, optional benefits, and stated tax/discount/loading assumptions.
- Show sourced premium ranges only with their exact profile/configuration, source, date and assumptions. Do not extrapolate a published illustration to another family, location, age, or SI.
- If no matching price evidence exists, say affordability cannot be verified. Do not estimate price from city, age, family size, or SI alone; show supported SI choices and ask the user to choose or provide/obtain a matching quote. Do not claim unknown prices fit budget (SPEC R9–R10).
- If all eligible matched-price choices exceed budget, show the closest source-priced choices as over budget, state the amount above budget, and ask whether to adjust budget or coverage. Never change either silently (R9).

## Suggestion rule

Within one eligible, source-verified product and structure, first keep choices that fit the confirmed family/health scenario. When both a user budget and exact matched premiums are known, **suggest the highest documented base SI whose matched quote is at or below that budget**; explain that this is the largest source-supported base amount within the stated budget, not a finding of adequacy, optimality, or likely claim payment. Show up to two other supported choices to make trade-offs visible.

When the budget is unknown or no matching quote is available, **suggest ₹20 lakh as the default starting amount only when that exact base-SI option is source-supported for the eligible product/variant/structure and fits the confirmed family/health rules**. State that affordability is unverified when there is no matching quote/budget and that ₹20 lakh is not an adequacy guarantee. Show up to two other supported choices. Do not replace ₹20 lakh with an unapproved “nearest” value. If ₹20 lakh is not offered, the only eligible option is a distinct unlimited option, or eligibility is unknown, show source-supported choices and say a default amount cannot be selected for that configuration; ask the user to choose or obtain a matching quote.

If a matching quote exists and a stated budget is known, use the highest-within-budget rule even when it produces an amount other than ₹20 lakh. If all matched priced options exceed budget, use R9; do not use the fallback to hide the over-budget result.

## Input validation boundaries

| Field | Accepted input / validation |
|---|---|
| Person's age | Explicit unknown or a whole-number age of 0 or greater. Reject negative, fractional, non-numeric, and non-finite values. Do not impose an arbitrary global maximum. Determine eligibility using the exact policy's documented entry-age limits and age basis; an age outside a plan's limit means that plan is not a match, not that the age/profile is malformed. |
| Annual budget | Explicit unknown or a finite non-negative INR amount with no more than two decimal places. Zero is a valid stated budget, not missing. Reject negative, non-numeric, non-finite, or over-precision values. A numeric amount is not a premium or an affordability claim. |
| Sum insured | Explicit unknown or a finite positive INR amount exactly matching an offered option for the selected product/variant/structure. Reject zero, negative, malformed, and unsupported amounts; show the actual supported choices for correction. Represent an official unlimited option with its source-backed label, not an invented numeric value. |
| Family/structure | Require per-person relationship and age inputs as specified in SPEC §10. Offer individual/floater/separate policies only when the exact product source supports that structure and member combination; never silently drop or add a person. |
