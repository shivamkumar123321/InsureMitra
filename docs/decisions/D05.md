# D05 — Minimal profile, health uncertainty, and plan structure

## Status

**Accepted; T07 verification and RD2 passed, 2026-10-26.** Profile and family-category behavior is concrete. Owner reviewed the D04–D06 checkpoint summary and replied “yes go ahead”; see [RD2 review evidence](../evidence/D05-RD2-review.md).

## Decision

### Minimal profile and proposer

- Do not ask for a nickname or first name to label a session. Use a neutral generated session label.
- The proposer is the adult (`age > 18`) who proposes/pays the premium and GST. An adult may propose for and be insured under their own single-person plan. This is the owner's app rule, not a universal IRDAI age requirement. With the existing completed-whole-year age input, the threshold is 19 or older.
- The proposer may be outside the insured member set. A person under 18 cannot be the proposer. For a minor's individual cover, capture a family adult over 18 as proposer, even if that adult is not insured. For other policies, the owner permits any adult to propose for another person; do not claim a particular insurer accepts an unrelated/non-insured proposer or third-party payer unless its proposal requirements support it. Otherwise mark proposer/payer acceptance as requiring insurer confirmation.
- Owner-defined "dependent" means a person covered by the plan who is not its proposer. Keep this role separate from an insurer's product-specific "dependent child" eligibility definition. For two or more proposed insured people under one policy, record each person's relationship to the proposer, age, and health state. A parent-child relationship also requires the parent's age to exceed the child's age by more than 18 years (`parent age - child age > 18`), as an owner-selected app rule.
- The owner's shared-plan close-family set is the insured person/proposer, spouse, own parents, and children. Uncle, aunt, and other extended relatives are excluded from that shared plan. A father-in-law may be considered for a separate plan but not added to the primary shared plan; other in-law/extended-relative coverage must also be structured separately. The proposer may be outside the insured set, but each separate arrangement must satisfy the exact product's terms.
- Follow each exact policy's count and child-age restrictions. The owner's examples include 2 adults/3 children, 2 adults/4 children, and child limits such as 25 or 30 years; these are examples, not global catalogue limits. Verify current product wording and UIN/variant for every arrangement.
- Before initial recommendations, display the complete editable profile and require explicit confirmation. For a policy to count as a verified match, the proposer rule, each proposed insured person's age, each required relationship, the parent-child age gap, and the product's count/age/structure limits must be known and fit. Unknown age or relationship keeps the affected policy outside the verified ranked shortlist (SPEC R2/R12).
- Budget, location, sum insured, or priority can remain explicitly unknown only where the applicable documented decision rule does not require the value. Continue to apply D03 and D04 when priority, budget, and coverage guidance are evaluated.

### Health states and follow-ups

- Keep three distinct states for each person: explicit “No conditions”; one or more user-reported condition names; or explicit “Not sure”. Preserve all known condition names even when the user is unsure whether their history is complete.
- A missing health answer remains missing and must be resolved as “No conditions”, named condition(s), or “Not sure” before profile confirmation. Never infer “No conditions” from silence or a skipped field (SPEC F1/§10).
- An explicit “Not sure” may continue provisionally as allowed by R3. Disclose affected health-suitability or eligibility uncertainty; never imply confirmed insurer acceptance.
- Ask a medical follow-up only when a named, current policy rule needs that exact detail. Record the policy source/rule. If the answer remains unknown, preserve that state and mark the affected plan/rule unverified; do not infer an answer or block otherwise-verifiable plans without a rule-based reason.
- Do not add general medical screening or collect diagnosis duration, treatment, medication, or other detail unless a documented rule for a considered plan requires it.

### Child age-out at renewal

- If the exact policy wording sets a dependent-child exit age and a covered child exceeds it by the next renewal, do not present that child as eligible to remain in that floater. Show a source-supported separate-policy or migration path and identify the transition timing.
- Do not describe every such transition as a fresh policy with lost continuity. IRDAI guidance says continuous health-policy renewal cannot be denied solely because of age and provides migration/portability routes; the exact product's wording and the insurer determine the child's available continuation route, eligibility, underwriting, and carried-forward credits. Ask the insurer to confirm where the product-specific route is unclear. See [renewal and migration evidence](../evidence/D05-irdai-floater-structure.md).
- Example: if a particular policy caps a dependent child at age 25, do not list a 26-year-old as remaining in that floater at renewal when its wording confirms the exit. Offer a source-backed separate policy/migration route and retain applicable continuity caveats.

### Family floater and split plan structure

- IRDAI describes a family floater as one shared sum insured usable by any or all members covered under that policy. IRDAI's public guidance does not provide an exhaustive, universal relationship whitelist for every family floater. It directs customers to check each insurer's product terms and states that one product need not serve every age/category/need. This conclusion about the lack of a universal roster is an inference from the official guidance, not a quotation of an explicit IRDAI sentence. See [official-source evidence](../evidence/D05-irdai-floater-structure.md).
- Do not call relatives “IRDAI-approved members” based on a global list. For every plan, verify the exact current policy wording/prospectus and matching UIN/variant for the permitted relationships, age limits, dependency requirements, member-count limits, and whether a family-floater structure is offered.
- If all requested people satisfy one product's documented family-floater rules, evaluate a single floater covering those people, with its shared sum insured and exact member list shown.
- If one floater cannot cover all requested people, offer separate source-supported policies or permitted floater groups that identify exactly who each covers. Do this only when the selected catalogue evidence supports every proposed member and structure. Do not silently omit a person, merge sums insured across policies, or claim an unverified combination covers the whole family. If no fully supported arrangement is found, say so and identify insurer confirmation as necessary.
- A father-in-law can be the proposer for a separate policy if he is older than 18; any other adult proposer may likewise be outside the insured set under the owner's rule. The insurer must still permit the proposer/payer arrangement for the selected product.
- No catalogue slot is certified by this decision. Per-product membership and structure verification remains with its assigned catalogue task; unknown or stale source evidence cannot establish eligibility.
