## Deterministic ranking rules

1. **Eligibility gate:** apply documented eligibility before ranking. Exclude documented-ineligible plans. Do not rank unresolved eligibility as a verified match; ask only the follow-up needed to resolve it. No AI-selected eligibility or score (SPEC R1–R3).
2. **Preferred insurer:** rank eligible plans from the preferred insurer. If it cannot meet the requirements, explain the gap and ask before presenting other insurers (R7).
3. **Main priority:** the user's explicit main priority dominates. Compare plans on that dimension first. Apply secondary preferences only as tie-breakers or refinements among equal-fit plans; explain any conflict. Never use weighted scoring.
4. **Lowest price:** for the lowest-price main priority, sort comparable known indicative premiums ascending even when a more expensive plan has fewer restrictions or broader coverage. Put unknown/non-comparable prices after known comparable premiums; do not claim cheapest or affordable for an unknown price (R5, R10).
5. **Fewer relevant restrictions:** compare restrictions material to the user's confirmed family/health scenario. Use a partial order: A ranks higher than B on this dimension only when A is no worse on every compared relevant restriction and better on at least one. Do not count restrictions or assign weights. If trade-offs make the plans incomparable, they remain equal-fit on this dimension; apply a secondary preference only if the user supplied one, then use R6's price tie-break when otherwise equally suitable.
6. **Fit for disclosed conditions:** prioritize documented eligibility and policy terms relevant to the disclosed health/family scenario. A plan that better fits that scenario ranks higher. Unknown or conflicting terms do not count as positive fit. Broader coverage may be displayed as a secondary benefit among equally scenario-fit options, but must not override scenario-specific fit.
7. **Unsure default:** lexicographic order: (a) documented family/health fit; (b) affordability; (c) fewer relevant restrictions. For affordability, known comparable within-budget premiums precede over-budget premiums and sort ascending within the applicable band. If all eligible known-priced options exceed budget, apply R9. Unknown premiums follow known prices without an affordability claim (R5, R9–R10).
8. **Ties:** plans equivalent on the main priority remain equally suitable unless a user-provided secondary preference distinguishes them. For otherwise equally suitable plans, lower comparable known premium may break the tie (R6). Equal prices, unknown prices, and non-comparable prices do not create another tie-break. Do not add arbitrary insurer, catalog order, or hidden score tie-breaks.
9. **Result count and budget:** return only verified eligible matches, target 2–3 without padding (R8). When every eligible known-priced option exceeds budget, show the closest priced options as over budget and ask whether to adjust budget or coverage (R9). Unknown premiums are never described as affordable (R10).
